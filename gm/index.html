CAMPAIGN STATE — AS OF SESSION FOUR

After “An Accord”, 26 August 2026.

1 · The premise

SET Togashi Norikage is 24, and Wrath of the Kami has not erupted once in his lifetime — a silence twenty-four years old and chronic, not one winter's failure. A priest at his cradle said the mountain would not wake while he lived. The Agasha have been living with the worst omen in their tradition for a generation. He is afraid the prophecy is true — not that it kills him, but that his life is a clock counting toward something that is building.

SOURCE writ-of-wilds-wilderness.lore — Wrath of the Kami is an active volcano at the center of Kinenkan province, causing tremors felt as far as the Centipede lands. Its annual winter eruption is so dependable that the Agasha consider it a troubling omen if it fails to erupt.

His ninjō is harder than it looks

SOURCE writ-of-wilds-wilderness.lore — The Phoenix have not yet revealed this to the other clans for political reasons.

His ninjō is to study with the Phoenix because they seem to understand the imbalance — but they are actively concealing it, so the goal is not to be taught. It is to get at a secret a Great Clan is deliberately keeping.

SOURCE The Kaito are the family deployed to investigate, they practice mikodo (the Way of Shrines), and they descend from the Hyoketsu, a Yobanjin tribe absorbed into the Phoenix in the 5th century. Their ancestral shrine Sanpuku Seido sits on the northern border in the Great Wall of the North and contains the well imprisoning Ateru's spirit, which weakened the boundary to Toshigoku.

The Phoenix most likely to teach him about neglected shrines are half the thing his Empire calls barbarian, and have their own containment problem. His ninjō and the Yobanjin thread are the same door.

4 · The disturbance

SET The chill Norikage felt on the road and the premonition are one phenomenon, not two. He is picking up a disturbance in the local earth kami. Its content: within a year, White Flower Village will be uninhabited or uninhabitable.

SET He knows roughly where it is. He does not know what it is, and Session Two did not change that — the column reached the village without the premonition acquiring a mechanism. The player named the problem exactly: a fact without a direction.

SOURCE writ-of-wilds-wilderness.lore — one leading theory of localized imbalance is the displacement of elemental spirits: kami taken from their enshrined places bring their elemental phenomena elsewhere, manifesting as natural disasters or unusual weather. Kami may be displaced when shrines fall into disrepair, when they are offended, or when encroaching spirits force them to flee.

SET Nothing physically sits at that spot on the road. There is no shrine, no boundary stone, no grave, no thing walking with the column — there is nothing there to find, and no scene to be had by going and looking. The location he fixed is where he felt it, not where an object is. Do not build a discovery there — two opportunity bought a bearing, and that is all they bought.

OPEN Are the kami leaving the abandoned villages? That chain would deliver “uninhabitable within a year” without a single grain of volcanic ash.

OPEN “Uninhabitable” implies a physical cause — ash, gas, fouled water, landslide. Still to be chosen, and now to be found at White Flower rather than on the way to it.

The pressure loop

Village empties → shrines go untended → kami are displaced → the ground gets strange → the mountain's behavior changes → the prophecy comes into question → Togashi authority erodes → the sect grows → more people leave.

SOURCE writ-of-wilds-wilderness.lore — the Kaito, deployed to investigate the imbalance, have been discovering many neglected or corrupted shrines.

SOURCE The theory that humans are the ultimate cause belongs to the Asako family: thoughtless interaction with natural resources has been slowly wearing away at elemental balance over generations. If accepted it would disrupt Rokugan's entire way of life.

5 · White Flower Village

Households with no shared history and no inherited order of precedence, expected to become one village by arriving in the same place — jostling for position with no settled basis to resolve it. SET 13 Aug The shrine north of the village is a Seidō to Fukurokujin, Fortune of Wisdom — by legend once mortal, mastered the way, learned to subsist on the breath of the universe rather than food and drink, and ascended. Martial artists venerate him; his holy symbol is the needle and tailors are devoted worshippers. He is said to know how to revive the dead and never to use or share it, for fear of misuse. SOURCE path-of-waves-ronin.lore — the sect holds that all are equal in the eyes of the Little Teacher, and that chanting “Shoshi ni kie” admits a person to the Perfect Land at death, bypassing the wheel.

SOURCE path-of-waves-hirosaka.lore — villages are prime recruitment targets precisely because daimyō take little interest in what villagers do as long as they pay taxes. Some daimyō who suspect sect control act ruthlessly, up to destroying entire villages.

SOURCE writ-of-wilds-dragon-clan.lore — the Agasha are less concerned about the Perfect Land Sect than other Dragon families, viewing it as a practical rather than spiritual problem. Seiya attitudes plausibly inherit this.

SOURCE emerald-empire-history.lore — late ninth century doctrine that the age is one of Declining Virtue and that chanting a mantra brings Shinsei's return. Offered peasants freedom from mortal trials and rebirth. Samurai considered it heretical; outlawed in Phoenix lands by the tenth century.

SOURCE There is a “Ties to the Perfect Land Sect” entry in writ-of-wilds-mechanics.ttrpg and a “Perfect Land Sect Member” in path-of-waves-systems.ttrpg.

The bind

The arrivals dilute the sect numerically at the moment they manufacture its best argument: the danger Norikage named is mixing, strangers with no inherited sense of who defers to whom, and the Perfect Land's answer to who defers to whom is nobody. A village suddenly full of strangers with no shared rites is where a portable, teacher-free doctrine takes root.

He said he would report the presence, not the person. In a bench of four that distinction collapses by arithmetic. He cannot keep both halves of his principle, and he does not know it yet.

SET 18 Aug The village took them in. Not by Sadao's decision and not on the daimyō's authority — Ume walked the line of newcomers, made up her mind, told Sadao “they're gonna stay here”, and waved the column past while he and Fusae were still deadlocked.

OPEN Whether the village keeps an everyday hokora distinct from the seidō, and who tends it. Nui leads the pilgrim road and is a Fortunist, not a convert, so the seidō is not in sect hands.

6 · The country

SET Where White Flower sits, and how far that is from Wrath of the Kami, is read off the Atlas. Its position is fixed and settled.

SOURCE The village has no lore text in the project files — a label on the map and nothing more.

The shape of the country

The Dragon lands do not rise and fall so much as fold. A traveller walks an hour along a slope of grey scree — loose broken rock, the kind that shifts underfoot — with a valley floor far below and no way down into it. Then the path turns and that valley is gone, replaced by another that looks nearly identical and lies in a different direction. This is why holdings a short distance apart as the crow flies can be days apart on foot, and why SOURCE the Agasha call their portion of these mountains the Twisting Labyrinth.

Water is the constant. Meltwater threads down every rock face, gathers into streams too loud to speak over, and falls in white ribbons that never appear to reach anything.

What is farmed, and what is not

There is virtually no flat ground, so the fields are terraces — narrow shelves cut into the hillsides and held up by dry stone retaining walls, each shelf perhaps two paces deep. They grow wheat and barley here rather than rice; the growing season is too short and the water too cold for paddy. Terraces require constant repair, because winter frost swells the ground and pushes the walls outward. A terrace that goes one season unattended slumps. A terrace that goes three is gone.

Perhaps one in three is worked. The rest have gone to thistle and stonecrop, their walls bowed and spilled. A farmhouse stands roofless in the middle of a field of volunteer barley — barley self-seeded from last year's unharvested crop, which grows thin and scattered and tells anyone who knows fields that no one gathered what stood there.

Animals

Goats, mostly, because goats are what this ground supports. Herds work ledges no wider than a hand, and their bells carry for a long way, sometimes the only human-made sound for an hour. Two boys minding forty goats between them is a detail that carries information: that is too many animals for two children, and it means the households behind them have no one else to send.

Ravens work the high air. Occasionally a hawk-eagle drops past below the level of the path. Woodsmoke arrives on the wind from valleys that never become visible.

Weather

It turns several times in a day. Cold sun, then a grey that swallows the far side of the valley, then thin driving sleet that stops as abruptly as it began, leaving the rock steaming where the sun finds it again. SOURCE Dragon winters in recent years have been the harshest in living memory — deep snows, avalanches — and the evidence is legible on the slopes: long tongues of raw stone and shattered pine below the treeline, too recent to have greened over.

The road east

YOURS The pilgrim road out of Fuchi Mura runs west toward the High House of Light, and it is maintained, because the clan cares what strangers make of the approach to the mountain. The eastern track toward White Flower Village is not maintained. It is an agreement between villages that a path exists. Within a mile it is the width of a goat.

The roadside shrines

Small wayside shrines are called hokora — a stone box with an open front, set at a bend in the road or beside a spring, housing a figure worn past identification. It might be a Fortune; it might be the kami of that particular spring. Nobody local necessarily distinguishes. These are the ordinary furniture of Rokugani roads and cost nothing to pass.

What is legible is which ones are kept. A tended hokora has a recent offering — a cedar sprig, river stones set in a deliberate row, something placed by hand within the last few days. An untended one has a season of leaf litter drifted onto the altar and its torii — the gate marking the boundary between ordinary ground and sacred ground — rotted through at the base and leaning.

The distinction matters more than it looks. SOURCE The Phoenix have quietly deployed the Kaito family across the Empire to investigate the elemental imbalance, and what they keep finding is neglected and corrupted shrines. One theory holds that kami abandon shrines that fall into disrepair or that offend them, and manifest their element somewhere else, as strange weather or worse. So a leaning torii on a mountain road is either an ordinary consequence of the people who tended it being moved elsewhere, or the beginning of something. From the road, both look the same.

NOTE This is the pressure loop from §4 made walkable, and it is Theology 2 / Meditation 2 territory — his actual competencies. Kiyo keeps her village's hokora; she is the natural witness for what happens to a shrine whose village leaves.

8 · What Norikage believes

  • Prophecy vs. the kami's will: if the mountain wakes tomorrow, the priest was not necessarily a liar. To hold that prophecy binds the heavens grants humanity a power that isn't ours.
  • On being a harbinger: people see the omen, not the man. Twenty-four years of Agasha unease have made him half-believe them.
  • Past lives: remembers none. Has flashes of competence he has no right to. Won't call himself a new soul — presumptuous — but: if the world were only the past rearranged, nothing new could occur, and things must sometimes be new.
  • His namesake: a brutal martial master. Offerings continue out of obligation, not love. He assumes the man would be embarrassed of him, and will not say the man was wrong.
  • Prayer: the side trail around an obstacle you cannot climb.
  • The Dragon and Kisshōten: need first, not faith first. He does not condemn it.
  • The Perfect Land Sect: subversive. If a mantra carries you past judgment without merit, the apathy that follows outweighs the good. Why would anyone do anything worth doing?
  • The Age of Declining Virtue: cannot be known until much later; saying it now only makes people readier to do nothing.
  • A villager who chants it: report the presence, not the person. Argue with her, not unkindly.
  • Whether the heresy is simply correct: he has refused to follow that road, and knows it. “It can't be true, therefore it isn't.”

NOTE His own words on each of these are in the Chronicle, Session One, under What he believes.

10 · House rules established in play

SET Off-approach distinctions. Portentous Birth is incentivised toward Fire, Affect of Harmlessness toward Air. Either may be used on an off-approach check, but grants one reroll die instead of two.

SET “Pick a door.” For some actions a menu of skills is offered, each revealing something different or expressing a different intent — choosing the skill is itself a choice about what the scene is about.

SET A session boundary clears strife the way a scene boundary does. Strife is reduced to half the character's Composure (rounded up) if it is over that, exactly as at scene end — it is not halved, and a character already at or under half Composure does not move. The GM may override and carry a full total into the next session when the fiction calls for it.

SET Elemental Deficiency (Fire) fires at the GM's call, in Fire stance. It does not trigger on every Fire-adjacent check. The GM decides when it applies, and absent a deliberate exception it is limited to checks made in Fire stance.

Session Five — arriving at the temple

SET He has two weeks of road behind him and an audience with Oharu in front of him, carrying three things nobody can verify: that two villages of Perfect Land devotees have been merged under a Bushidō-keeping head, that White Flower will not last a year, and that trolls are people.

NOTE The report is the decision. Lord Togashi's Insight already told him what happens if the daimyō learns of the sect — the village does not need its year. Oharu is not the daimyō and does not answer to him, which is exactly why Norikage chose to walk here rather than tell Fusae. What he says in this room decides whether that lever ever gets pulled, and by whom.

NOTE Oharu holds status 45 against Seiya Mori's 65. An abbot of the Tattooed Order can seek an audience with a provincial daimyō and be heard; he cannot instruct him, and nothing obliges him to carry anything upward. Whatever Norikage hands him, Oharu chooses what becomes of it.

OPEN Whether the temple already knows about the troll, the road being the pilgrim route north.

Still to decide

Tōbei

OPEN Tōbei has no tarot card, no trade and no age. Also open: whether the child's death is common knowledge in the village or something he and Ume carry privately.

MINE Tōbei: water. In a village of terraces the meltwater is allocated by custom, and he is the custom — he says whose shelf drinks first and in what order down the hillside, and has settled every water dispute White Flower has had without once involving a samurai.

MINE His birthplace: split it from the posting — born in a Kinenkan mountain village near the volcano, so that he learns about the mountain late and secondhand, which is how he learns everything else.
