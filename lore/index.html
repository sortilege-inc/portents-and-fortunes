The Emerald Empire

Lore of Rokugan

The world beneath the chronicle — the order of Heaven, the code of the samurai, and the Dragon who watches from the peaks.

The Dragon Clan

Descended from the Kami Togashi — the prophet who saw the outcome of the Tournament of the Kami and, by that sight, excused himself from it — the Dragon are reckoned by the rest of the Empire its least legible clan. They keep to the high mountains, prefer insight to conquest, and tolerate, sometimes prize, the kind of criticism another clan would name insubordination; to a Dragon, unquestioning loyalty is not loyalty. Four families divide that character between them: the Mirumoto, duellists and administrators, who hold the rugged foothills and the mines in them; the Agasha, alchemists and shugenja; the Kitsuki, investigators, who steward the flattest and most fertile ground the clan possesses; and the Togashi, who are not a bloodline at all but a monastic order.

The Togashi Tattooed Order

The monks of the Togashi — the ise zumi — are ascetics and mystics who bear mystical tattoos, marks said to be inked with the blood of the Kami himself, which empower their kihō, the disciplines of body and ki. Under the Order's teaching of Blood of the Kami, a monk takes a tattoo of some natural motif, links it to a chosen technique, and gains further marks as understanding deepens. The ise zumi are also held to remember their past lives, and they record history as they have witnessed it across successive ones — an archive strikingly objective and hopelessly fragmentary. Membership is by individual calling rather than birth, and the Order accepts that some of its monks will follow paths so divergent as to make them outcasts in all but name. Occasionally the Kami Togashi is said to visit a descendant in a flash of vision, or an insight that arrives unaccounted for. Whether that is a gift or a burden the Order's scholars have not settled.

The Elemental Imbalance

Something has gone wrong with the five elements. The Phoenix noticed first, when their cold and rainy provinces went to drought and simple fire invocations began erupting into infernos. The pattern has since widened: Lion harvests slightly down with the summer rains, a long-dormant Scorpion volcano waking without warning, Dragon winters the harshest in living memory. In the Dragon lands the sign is a mountain that will not erupt and snow that does not stop.

The Phoenix have not told the other clans, for political reasons. They have deployed the Kaito family to investigate, and what the Kaito keep finding are neglected and corrupted shrines. One account holds that kami abandon shrines that fall into disrepair or that give offence, and manifest their element somewhere else — as strange weather, or worse. The Asako hold something harder to say aloud: that generations of thoughtless human use have worn the balance thin. Accepted, that would unmake the Empire's way of life, which is reason enough that it is not much spoken.

Bushidō

The code names the virtues by which a samurai's life is measured — among them Sincerity, Duty and Loyalty, Compassion, Courage, Courtesy, Honor, and Righteousness. Few hold all of them perfectly at once, and a samurai's character is usually legible in which tenet they hold paramount and which they hold least. Norikage places Sincerity above all and Duty lowest. The ordering has cost him nothing so far, because nothing has yet asked him to lie.

The Celestial Order

Heaven, Earth, and the mortal realm are bound in one order, watched over by Lord Sun and Lady Moon and the Fortunes and kami between them. Omens are read as that order's language — a silent mountain, a mark on a newborn, a shrine gone cold — and the reading belongs to theologians, shugenja, and monks, who serve the Empire as its interpreters of Heaven's will. It is the one art in which Norikage is unambiguously competent.
